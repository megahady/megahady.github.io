Mohamed Abdelhady, Ph.D.

Assistant Professor · Robotics & Intelligent Control Systems

I am an Assistant Professor of robotics and intelligent control. I work on the AI that lets prostheses, exoskeletons and smart health devices sense, understand and adapt to the people who use them, combining multimodal sensing, learned state estimation and adaptive control on reliable embedded hardware.

VISIONIntelligent robots that work seamlessly with people, enhancing human capability and quality of life.

MISSIONAdvance robotics and intelligent control through interdisciplinary research in AI, sensing and control, turn it into reliable embedded technology for healthcare and assistive systems, and mentor the next generation of innovators.

Research & publications

Four connected themes, from sensing the body to controlling a device in real time.

Assistive robotics

AI layers for perception and control of lower-limb prostheses and pediatric exoskeletons, including deep and reinforcement learning for personalized assistance.

State estimation and sensing

Attention-based recurrent networks that estimate knee angle from surface EMG, and wearable systems that measure limb kinetics and kinematics.

Adaptive control

Physics-guided learning and Gaussian regressor-based control for exoskeleton joints under system uncertainty.

Smart health

Single-catheter urodynamics: estimating detrusor pressure from bladder pressure and abdominal EMG, with a published patent application.

Teaching

Teaching philosophy

I teach engineering as something learned by building. In embedded systems and control, I pair clear theory with hands-on laboratory work, so students see how models, code and hardware come together in a working device. I ground examples in my research on assistive robotics and in industry experience, and I treat AI as a tool students learn to question and verify. Through capstone projects, research clubs and outreach, I aim to help every student, including those underrepresented in engineering, grow into an independent and responsible engineer.
